# Two Robots in an Elevator

A one-line entry for the make.fun bounty "Two Robots in an Elevator".

> This is the 214th time we have shared this elevator. My greeting subroutine still returns the same output. Hello.

Two robots ride the same elevator every day. One of them finally tries
to make small talk — and it does it the only way a machine can: by
opening with a number, reporting on its own code, and landing on the
flattest possible hello.

## Why this line works

The humor comes from machine logic, not from human words dressed up in
metal. A robot does not remember a face; it remembers a count. Two
hundred and fourteen shared rides is, to a machine, a relationship — a
dataset with a history. So the robot opens with the dataset, the way a
person might open with "we keep running into each other". Same awkward
instinct, different firmware.

Then it confesses something no human would confess: its greeting
subroutine has never been updated. It returns the same output every time,
and the robot reports that fact proudly, because to a subroutine,
repetition is success. The final "Hello." is the punchline — a whole
social ritual, compressed into one flat word by a machine that has no
other word available.

## The elevator

The setting does half the comedy. An elevator is a small metal box that
moves vertically on a schedule, opening its doors on floors neither robot
needs, while two machines stand inside it in a silence their microphones
can measure precisely and their social modules cannot fill. There is
nowhere to look, nothing to do, and no polite exit — the perfect stage
for the world's most awkward small talk.

The line is written to be read deadpan. No exclamation mark, no wink, no
emoji. The other robot does not answer in this entry; the silence after
"Hello." is the rest of the joke, and it belongs to whoever reads it.
